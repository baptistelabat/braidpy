Making a braid on a marudai

Braid Studio makes a disk braid — a kumihimo or mobidai braid, an Ashley sinnet, a braid word rolled onto a ring of bobbins — move by move, as it would be made on a marudai: each moved yarn carried over the others to its new place and drawn tight, the braid settling below. web/marudai.js does it in the browser, and braidpy.marudai is the same algorithm in Python, to read and to check it by.

The method is that of braid3dmin, a kumihimo simulation in three.js: yarns as chains of beads, pulled by their bobbins, relaxed a bead at a time. This page describes braidpy’s own implementation of it, in its own terms; the code is this project’s own, and none of braid3dmin’s is in it.

The yarns

Each yarn is a chain of beads a unit apart and two units thick: a unit is half a yarn diameter. Inside, lengths are in units and y is up; what comes out is in yarn diameters, z up.

  • A link between two beads of a yarn pulls them together only when it is stretched beyond a unit, as a rope does: a slack yarn pushes nothing.

  • Two beads less than two units apart, of the same yarn or not, push each other apart. Links and contacts have the same stiffness (10).

  • A yarn’s end is drawn towards its carrier, on the rim of the marudai’s mirror and above the braid’s tip, by a constant pull (1.2): its bobbin. The carriers stand on a circle of radius 19 units, half the rim’s reach (20) above the tip.

A move

A move takes a yarn’s end from its carrier to another, round the rim:

  1. Laid over the top. The yarn’s new stretch goes from where it leaves the braid up over everything in its way — a map of the yarns’ heights says how high — and down to its new carrier, a bead a unit.

  2. Drawn tight. The new stretch is then drawn as a string would go: each bead moved to between its neighbours or, where a yarn is in the way, round it, to whichever side has room.

  3. Relaxed. The beads that moved are queued. One at a time, each is moved by the forces on it — its links, its contacts, its bobbin — by a step that shrinks as the relaxation goes on, and its neighbours and the beads near it are queued in turn. It stops when the queue dies down.

A move of more than nine tenths of a half turn goes in two halves, so that it passes over what lies on its own side of the braid. When every yarn moves alike, the disk itself turns: the braid turns with it, or, held, the bobbins turn round it and twist the yarns in.

The braid

  • Turning to balance. Hanging free, after each move the braid turns about its axis until its yarns’ ends pull it no way round: the angle is found by a search up to an eighth of a turn either way.

  • More than 30 yarns. The method was made for marudai braids of a few dozen yarns at most. Beyond 30, two things change, and braids of 30 yarns or fewer are made exactly as before:

    • The yarns are tied round rings of at most 30, one ring 2 units under the next, the yarns taken in turn round the disk. A single ring, a third of a unit across per yarn, would be so wide that the yarns rose from it as a hollow tube and were never drawn into a braid.

    • Each yarn’s pull on the braid is taken along its whole way, from the braid’s tip out to its end, instead of along its last link alone. A link just laid can point any way, and with so many yarns the braid swung back and forth by more each move, then grew without end.

    ABOK #3063, #3077, #3079 and #3081 (31 to 45 strands) are then made a few diameters long. #3082 (61 strands) still is not: 61 yarns side by side do not fit round the carriers’ circle.

  • Made, frozen. Well behind each yarn’s end — 40 beads behind the last that moved — the braid is made: its beads are frozen, and only the newest rows move. A frozen bead moves only as the whole braid turns.

  • Neighbours found in a grid. Beads are kept in flat arrays, and a bead’s neighbours are found in a grid of cells 2.6 units wide; the made braid, well below anything free, leaves the grid.

Round a core

A braid can be made round a core (job.core, "yarn" or "rigid"): a chain of beads up the braid’s axis, from where the braid begins to 20 units above its tip, past the carriers, kept growing as the braid grows. This is braidpy’s own; braid3dmin has no core, and a braid saved for it is saved without one.

  • Of yarn, its end is drawn straight up, and it gives way to the yarns pressing on it; its beads freeze as the braid is made round them. Rigid, every bead is held where it stands.

  • Passed beside. The core is left out of the heights a yarn is carried over, so a moved yarn goes past it, not over its top. A move of more than nine tenths of a half turn is made in two halves already, so a yarn goes round the core on the side it is moving to. It is then drawn tight round the core, as round any yarn in its way, and pushed off it as off any other.

  • Turning about it. The braid turns to balance about the core, the axis, rather than about its yarns’ ends.

  • The yarns are tied clear of it: a tenth further from it than touching, as they are tied clear of each other.

The core comes after the yarns in everything returned, and has no carrier: the carriers’ circle is the yarns’ alone.

What comes out

The yarns, cut at the braid’s tip, and their tails from the tip out to their carriers; how far the braid has turned; and, for Grow, the moments of the making — each yarn carried over, drawn tight, the braid settled — kept as their beads still free (Braid Studio).

How it compares with braid3dmin run on the same moves, and with the other ways Braid Studio settles a braid, is in Settling a braid by physics, under “Made on a marudai, move by move”.
