Changelog

What changed in each release of braidpy.

0.4.0 — 2026-11-27

braidpy tells braids apart by what they are, and makes them more ways. Four kinds of “the same braid” — the same word, the same braid, the same pattern repeated, the same knot or link — each with its own ==; normal forms of its own, Artin’s and the band generators’, so math-braid is no longer needed; whether a braid is periodic, reducible or pseudo-Anosov. Horn gear machines are written as their graphs, with split gears, cores and axials anywhere, sized for their bobbins; a tube is made as a tube on a lace machine; disk moves are made in band letters where that is quicker; Ashley’s sinnets come from the shape they take, and 37 more of his plat sinnets join the catalogue. Braid Studio speaks French, makes braids round a core, and steps through a disk’s or a sinnet’s moves. braidpy’s names now take American spelling (color, center, catalog), the old ones still answering, with a warning.

New

  • 37 more of Ashley’s plat sinnets as braid words (braid_catalog.ASHLEY_PLATS, #2976–#3019): French, flat, twill, duplex and doubled-edge sinnets led to the centre, and square, half-round, ellipse and round sinnets by the square sinnet’s method, each written as the book gives its leads (“u3 o1”, under three, over one) and made by AshleyPlat. What the book says of them holds: #3018 is #3001, #2989 divided equally is #3003 turned end for end, and #2999, #2965, #2967 and #2968 are the sinnets already catalogued.

  • Which of periodic, reducible or pseudo-Anosov a braid is, on Braid Studio’s card and in tools.describe_braid (nielsen_thurston): said of braids of few strands and crossings, the super summit set looked through only so far — past that, the card says it cannot tell.

  • Whether a braid is reducible (Braid.is_reducible): some of its strands gathered into groups, fewer groups than strands, each running inside one tube, the tubes themselves free to braid — a round curve round consecutive strands, two of them at least, all but two at most, brought back to itself. Asked of the super summit set conjugacy computes, a reducible braid having a conjugate there that keeps a round curve (Benardete, Gutiérrez and Nitecki 1993; Calvez 2014, theorem 10). With Braid.is_periodic, this says which of periodic, reducible or pseudo-Anosov a braid is.

  • A machine’s braid as a ring word round an axis (braidpy.horn_gear.word.ring_word): an axial, or any spot one could be fed at, the core the machine braids round if not said (default_axis). The turns are kept, so with the core kept as a strand (annulus_braid.cored_word) it is the braid the machine makes round a yarn fed there: a tube is not a flat braid curled up. shortened() shortens it, the relations read round the ring (shortening.shortened_ring, annulus_braid.turns_last).

  • A tube made as a tube on a lace machine (making.lace.ring_card): a ring word’s strands go round across the seam, a pass need not bring them home, and the card’s braid round the core is the word’s. A machine’s tube is the fewest steps that repeat (horn_gear.word.ring_unit): 2 of the 16-carrier tubular machine’s 16. Braid Studio’s Make it as… makes a braid made round a core so — a ring machine’s, a ring word’s — in far fewer steps (that machine: 5 a pass instead of 34), round a core of yarn. RingProgram.ring_word reads a program round its ring.

  • Four kinds of “the same braid”, each with its own == and hash (braidpy.braid_word, braid_pattern, closed_braid): BraidWord (braid.word()), the same crossings; Braid, the same braid; BraidPattern (braid.pattern()), the same braid made over and over, proven by conjugacy where it can be; and ClosedBraid (braid.closed()), the same knot or link, by its loops, linking numbers and Alexander polynomial, however many strands. conjugacy.same_braid is now same_pattern, which is what it tells; the old name is kept. The topology catalogue groups braids by pattern key, with the same 91 topologies as before.

  • Catalogues from outside: a package of a site’s own adds braids to braidpy’s catalogues (braidpy.catalog.extended, entry points in the group braidpy.catalogues), as braid-designer adds machines of its own without braidpy knowing them. A profile’s packages name the site’s packages: the build runs braidpy with them, installs their wheels in the page, and merges the words of their braids from their own locales/. A profile must now give packages.

  • Braid Studio in French, or another language: a profile’s language (web/profile.toml) names the catalogue its pages are shown in, web/locales/fr.toml for French, each text braidpy writes in English, in Python or in the page, put into it as it is shown (web/i18n.js). A site opens in its profile’s language, braidpy’s in English, braid-designer’s in French with language = "fr"; a menu at the top of each page switches between them there and then, the braid on show kept, and remembers the choice (or ?lang=fr in a link). A profile must now give language.

  • A design saved by braid3d (.b3d) read as a mobidai braid (braidpy.b3d.mobidai_from_b3d, braidpy.web.from_b3d): its yarns in their colours, its moves and its repeat, on a disk of as few slots as hold its yarns. In Braid Studio, Import a braid3d design under the mobidai braids fills your own with it and makes it. Ashley’s #3054, as designed in braid3d, is the sinnet the catalogue has from his drawing; disk braids and sinnets saved for braid3d read back as the same braid. braidpy.b3d is braidpy’s one reader of the format (read_b3d, write_b3d): braidpy.craft_design_online and its BraidModel are gone, and with them the dependency on dataclasses-json.

  • A braid saved for braid3dmin writes, with each step, where the yarns are before it, as braid3dmin’s own files do; and a strand moved half a turn or more, as kumihimo takes one across the disk, as two steps each less than half a turn, since a move’s angles say only the short way round: it was written going the other way round. The angle of a disk’s slot is one rule, slotAngle in web/marudai.js (braidpy.marudai.slot_angle), which web/b3d.js and braidpy.b3d use.

  • Braid Studio built under another name, crediting and linking otherwise: what the site calls itself, whom it credits, where it links and whether search engines are welcome are a profile (web/profile.toml), and web/build.py --profile builds the site with another. With braidpy’s own, the pages are as before. How a private site would build on braidpy without forking it: A site of your own in the developer guide. The studio no longer names braidpy while loading, nor in Also made as.

  • The catalogue’s horn gear machines written as their graphs, as a machine of your own is (machine_catalog): the gears that mesh, where they feed axial yarns, and, should it be given one, the carriers it starts with (machine_from_graph(..., axials=, loading=), described.read_axials, read_loading, BraidingMachine.with_loading). horn_gear.examples builds them so. Each is the machine it was, with as many carriers, but its slots may be numbered from another one round and it loads itself: the tubular braids of 12 and 16 now start on other slots, and a loading written for a machine as it was may load it otherwise. The machines braiding several bands at once are built as before. A soutache’s or princess braid’s cords are named after their gear (axial_A).

  • A machine written as its graph puts a gear’s contacts where the layout has them whenever whole slots can say so exactly, and only otherwise as evenly as its slots allow: the ring of four gears now has each gear’s contacts a slot apart, as they are, rather than two.

  • Every place a horn gear machine could feed an axial yarn (layout.axial_spots: up each gear, down each ring of gears with room), drawn in Braid Studio on the machine: a click loads a spool there or takes it off (Axial yarns, axials in a link, none for none).

  • Split gears in a machine of your own: a gear split into parts on one axle, written A-B1 B2-C B1+B2, in two parts or more (H1+H2+H3), anywhere in the graph; each part’s neighbour stands where that part’s horns come round. Every catalogued machine, the twin plait and the three-band machine among them, is written as its graph, and “Your own machine…” starts from any. A tree of gears now branches round a gear meshing with three or more, rather than piling them on one another; and a machine’s yarns are drawn off fast enough that two passing the same point, as a split gear’s parts’ carriers do, are a diameter apart.

  • A machine braiding round cores has a braid word: each core is a strand of it, standing still where it rises through the deck, which the carriers pass in front of or behind (horn_gear.word.flat_word, annular_word; they refused such machines). Soutache, princess and the triaxial braid now have their word, crossings and permutation in Braid Studio, and join the catalogue of braids by topology. Soutache’s word read off the machine is the one read off its yarns hanging in space, cords and all.

  • Any horn gear machine, written as the gears that mesh: A-B B-C C-D D-A, a gear with its own slots A:6 (horn_gear.described.machine_from_graph; graph_of writes a machine back so). Each gear’s direction and contacts are worked out from the graph; a ring of an odd number of gears, or gears in separate machines, are refused saying why. In Braid Studio, Your own machine…, starting from the machine chosen before. Studio now reads a machine’s braid round its ring whenever its gears make one, not by its name.

  • A machine’s carrier tracks in Braid Studio, as the demo draws them: on the machine to load, and in the view of it turning, each track in a colour of its own (horn_gear.visualization.track_curves, which the demo now draws its tracks with too). A machine’s braid gives them in its timeline (tracks); otherwise every build is as before.

  • Braid Studio’s front page: every braid it offers as it comes, in a table to filter by name, by what makes it, by strands, by the loops it closes into and by writhe, each with a picture of it settled, its links (the book, videos, pages) and what else makes the same braid, and opened in the studio at once (braidpy.web.braids, web/home.js, web/snapshots.py). The studio itself is now make.html; old addresses naming a braid go on there.

  • Each catalogued braid’s links, as data: where it comes from and where it is shown made, each a title, a kind (book, article, video, page, post) and a web address (catalog.Link, Entry.links; Entry.reference is now the first link’s title). The addresses that were in the catalogues’ docstrings and notes are there now, and go out with the topology catalogue (topology_catalog.export), for Braid Studio to show.

  • Tightening a braid as a window on an endless one rather than a piece held at both ends (take_off.Periodic, tighten_yarns(periodic=...)): the yarn leaving the fell end of the window runs on into the top of it, so the braid is not pinched where it was held. The window must be a whole number of periods; take_off.Periodic.closing finds the wrap a laid braid closes on. Without it, tightening is as before. In Braid Studio, Sideways, endless under Settle the yarns (web/tighten.js, tested against braidpy step for step).

  • Whether a braid is Brunnian: pure, and trivial once any one of its strands is taken out, as the Borromean rings’ braid (Braid.is_brunnian, with Braid.without_strand; it raised NotImplementedError). Braid Studio’s card says so of a pure braid.

  • A braid made by its carriers (braidpy.carried): a horn gear machine’s carriers go round at the machine’s real size, each pulling its yarn at a steady tension up through a forming ring into the braid taken off above, the yarns held where they cross by friction. Drawn near, and to scale beside the machine (examples/demo_carried.py).

  • A triaxial braid among the machines: the tubular braid of 16 round an axial yarn up each of its eight gears, as a triaxial braider feeds them up its hollow horn gear spindles (horn_gear.triaxial_braid_16; J. H. van Ravenhorst’s thesis on the references page). At every axial the carriers going round one way pass it on one side and those going the other way on the other, so it lies between the two sets. Braid Studio says how many cores a machine braids round, where it said “a core” of the soutache’s two cords and now these eight.

  • A kumihimo or mobidai braid can be made round a core in Braid Studio, down the disk’s middle, of yarn or rigid, as a machine’s core is.

  • The marudai makes a braid round a core: up the braid’s axis, of yarn or rigid, each move passing beside it (braidpy.marudai, web/marudai.js). A disk braid, a lace braid made from its card, or a lace machine’s round its core, is made on the marudai with it.

  • A tubular machine, or a lace machine of six gears or more, can braid round a core in Braid Studio, of yarn or rigid, down the hole its ring of gears encloses (horn_gear.ring_core); soutache can braid without its cords. Core replaces Cores, which said only how stiff a machine’s own cores were: links made with it make the same braid.

  • A lace machine’s braid can be made on a marudai in Braid Studio: the braid grows at the fell as the card turns the gears, each gear’s turn its two notches swapping bobbins. Worked out from the card alone (making.lace.card_program), without building the machine or loading what it needs.

  • The multiband machines draw every carrier of a band in that band’s colour, so the bands can be told apart in the braid they make together; a band shown alone keeps its colour (BraidingMachine.carrier_colors).

  • A line of horn gears that braids three bands at once: the 10-15-10 machine of the Atelier de Tressage (braidpy.horn_gear.multiband). Where two bands meet, a six-horn gear is split between them, three horns each, so each band turns its carriers back on a gear of its own and the bands never meet. Its 35 carriers run the whole machine, shown in Braid Studio whole or a band at a time, with the smallest such machine beside it: two plaits braided at once on three gears, the middle one split.

  • Braid Studio’s Make it as… makes the braid on a lace machine too: the card it shows is a link to your own lace machine, loaded with that card and its bobbins, at an address of its own. Bobbins can be named by their notch, A-B, as the card names them.

  • Four kumihimo disk braids shared by Richard Buenger: edo yatsu, naiki, hira-naiki and uneri-maru-genji (braidpy.mobidai_catalog).

  • Braid Studio steps through a disk’s or a sinnet’s moves, as through a machine’s: ⏮ ◀ ▶ ⏭ or the caret in the moves typed show the strands after any move, without undoing it. A move clicked goes in at the cursor.

  • Whether two braids are the same braid however written, proven rather than measured: braidpy.conjugacy.same_braid decides conjugacy by the super summit set. It proves edo yatsu and ABOK #3070 different, and the flat braid of 6 and ABOK #3021.

  • A sinnet from the shape it is to take, as Ashley draws it (braidpy.making.ashley.sinnet_from_shape): lines across the shape parallel to its sides, turning at the outline in bights, the bights the stand’s spaces and the lines its moves, in the book’s order or space by space. Triangles are the book’s #3045, #3047, #3051 and #3059 exactly, #3049 with a strand more at each corner (corner_spaces), #3057 made space by space; flat oblongs, with their chisel edges, #3071 and #3072. A shape may turn back, as an L or a U, its lines cut in pieces, or have a hole, its bights numbered with the outline’s, an inner one between two outer ones, so that the sinnet goes round the hole as a tube does. Its tracks (shape_tracks) can each be made the other way round. On a grid, a shape is its cells, squares, squares cut on their diagonals or triangles (grid_shape, grid_outline); on the diagonal grid its outline may run on a slant, its lines across and up (grid_lines): a square standing on a corner has the lines of #3073 and #3074, an oblong on a corner those of #3075.

  • And back, from a sinnet’s moves to its shape: the moves grouped by the direction of their lines (move_groups), the same at any size for shapes drawn on one principle, say the grid, and their number the size; the shapes of that grid with as many lines are kept if they join the very spaces the moves join (shapes_from_moves). Every shape on a grid comes back so, and the book’s #3045 to #3051, #3059 and #3070 to #3075. The book’s sinnets that a shape draws say so in the catalogue (solid_sinnets_catalog.DRAWN), and Braid Studio’s list of shapes’ braids is made from it.

  • In Braid Studio, Ashley’s method, from a shape: a grid, square, diamond or hexagonal, and a shape it offers to start from (an oblong, an L, a U, a hollow square; a square or an oblong on a corner; a triangle or a hexagon), or one of thirteen braids made so; drawn once made, its cells, tracks and spaces clicked to change the shape, turn a track round or add a strand.

  • Two split gears may mesh once for each pairing of their parts (A1-B1 A2-B2 A1+A2 B1+B2), as a matrix of split gears wants: they touch at one contact, the pairing named first handing over at the end of each step, the next part way through it (Connection.phase). A carrier handed on twice in a step is stopped where it meets another.

  • A machine is sized for its bobbins: drawn larger, sizes and places together, it keeps its carriers further apart in proportion (horn_gear.take_off.clearance, Clearance.scale_for). Braid Studio sizes every machine for bobbins of 20 mm (Machine.bobbin_diameter_mm) and says how large its horns, gears and deck come out; it stops a machine only where two carriers come to one point (horn_gear.take_off.too_close), which no size keeps apart.

Changed

  • braidpy’s names are spelled in American English, as Python’s and its libraries’ are (color, center, neighbor, catalog) — one rule, no exceptions; prose may be British. A test keeps it so (tests/test_spelling.py). Renamed, the old names still answering with a DeprecationWarning (braidpy._renamed), each for the release that renames it and one more: ashley_solid_sinnet.strand_colors, horn_gear.visualization.colored_tracks, horn_gear.word.deck_center, BraidingMachine.carrier_colors, StrandTrajectories.center, DescribedMachine(centers=) and its centers, the color= of BraidGrowth.traces, and the attributes b3d.Design.colors, CarriedBraid.colors and AnnularWord.center (built with the old keyword, these three no longer are); the module braidpy.catalogue and web.catalogue() are braidpy.catalog and web.catalog(), the topology catalogue’s catalogue() is catalog(), similarity’s catalogue_braids() is catalog_braids(), the mobidai catalogue’s CataloguedMobidai and CataloguedBraid are CatalogedMobidai and CatalogedBraid, and each catalogue’s CATALOGUE is CATALOG; the catalogue modules keep the names 0.3.0 shipped them — braid_catalog, machine_catalog, mobidai_catalog, solid_sinnets_catalog and topology_catalog. Braid Studio’s links and the JSON its pages serve are unchanged, but the site writes its catalog as catalog.json, keeping catalogue.json beside it for the sites built before it (a few months, a release at least), each to be dropped in a later one.

  • Braids are compared as patterns (braidpy.braid_pattern) where braidpy compares them: BraidPattern.alike, and braid_pattern.alike(a, b) for two braids, say nothing measured tells two patterns apart — fast, the fingerprints — where == proves it. The ways of making a braid, the Ashley search and similarity.similar check by it, with the same results as before. ring_program.same_braid is its old name, kept. tools.compare_braids, and so the MCP server, now also says whether two braids are proven the same pattern.

  • Braids in band generators, braidpy’s own (braidpy.band_canonical_form): a_ts, strands t and s crossing in front of those between, as round a kumihimo disk or a marudai. Birman, Ko and Lee’s normal form (Braid.get_band_canonical_factors, before math-braid’s: the same, checked against it, faster) and a braid as band letters (Braid.band_word, artin_from_band). Disk braids spell far shorter so: Kongo gumi on 8 strands, 81 crossings, is 43 band letters; the 16-strand disk braid, 351, is 117. Braid Studio’s card gives a braid’s band word where it is shorter than its word, as a disk braid’s is, and Artin’s word elsewhere; a band word can be typed too (layout band: 4:1 1:3), and the MCP server’s describe_braid gives it.

  • A braid is made on a kumihimo disk in band letters where that takes fewer moves (making.disk.disk_moves): two strands far apart cross in three moves, lifted over those between, where crossings of neighbours take three each. Kongo gumi takes 129 moves instead of 243, the 12-strand disk braid 261 instead of 567, the 8-carrier tubular machine’s 78 instead of 96; a braid no shorter so, as a flat machine’s, is made as before, move for move. band_canonical_form.band_letters reads a word as band letters as it stands.

  • braidpy no longer needs math-braid (PyPI 0.8, 2018, unmaintained): a braid’s triviality, and so ==, falls back on braidpy’s own Garside normal form when handle reduction takes too long, and the page no longer installs it; the tests still check braidpy against it. The normal form (artin_left_normal_form, left_weighted) is the same, worked out another way: the half twists kept at the word’s end, each factor made left-weighted with those before it in one pass back, the crossings each can begin and end with changed only where a crossing moves. Fifty times faster: ABOK #3082 on 61 strands against itself rewritten, three seconds (math-braid: seventeen).

  • In a ring of gears timed so that every contact is on a horn (described, layout.time_ring), the parts of a split gear are stacked on their axle, each keeping its own time, the axle as large as one part; the bands they join are laid out each alone and put together, each split gear’s parts half a pitch apart (or j/k of k). Two matrices of gears joined by split gears, the split ladder, now each braid as they do alone: 28 carriers rather than 14, clear of each other. Machines without split gears in a ring are as before.

  • A sinnet whose strands all move to the right (AshleySolidSinnet.all_right), as the book’s #3058, #3059 and #3066 to #3069 do: a strand from an even space is led anticlockwise too, still from the left of its space. The disk, its animation and the mobidai pattern go round as the book says; the braid is the same, a strand carried over all being free to swing over the top. #3057 and #3058, given one table in the book, now differ as the book has them, in this alone.

  • Every contact of a horn gear machine hands over when a horn of each gear comes round to it: round a ring of six or eight gears of four slots, or a matrix of gears of several sizes, the gears are moved and sized so that they do (layout.time_ring), a contact handing over part way through a step where it must. tubular_12, tubular_16 and triaxial_16 build so, with the same carriers, period and braid.

  • Gears in a ring are drawn to touch, those that do not mesh kept clear (layout.ring_geometry), rather than shrunk from a spring layout till no mesh was too long, which left gears of several sizes running through each other.

  • A quantity not in SI units carries its unit in its name: making.ashley_sinnet and making.sinnet_for_page take time_limit_s.

  • Leftovers removed: braidpy.operations (its one function, conjugate(b, c), is c * b * c.inverse()), braidpy.material_braid (an early sketch; yarns with a thickness are take_off’s and rope’s), the empty braid_catalog.triangle_sinnet_9 and triangle_sinnet_10 (ABOK #3057, #3058 and #3045 are in solid_sinnets_catalog), and src/braidpy/analytic/, which held only a pointer to Why there is no closed form.

  • braidpy.take_off is a package rather than one module of 1734 lines: the trajectories laid and the yarns (paths), laying (laying), contact between yarns (contact), tightening (tighten), drawing (drawing), where each source’s strands go (sources) and a disk’s braid laid round a ring (ring). Every name is imported from braidpy.take_off as before, and every braid Braid Studio makes with the site comes out the same.

Fixed

  • The nearness of a band’s new axles to those placed, on a machine of split gears, comes out a plain float: it was numpy’s, as np.linalg.norm gave it.

  • Braid.is_periodic() answers the theorem it quoted: a braid is periodic when its nth power or its (n − 1)st power is a power of the half-twist Δ, read in the Garside canonical form. It asked only whether such a power was pure, so σ₁² kept every strand’s place and was said periodic, as was the catalogue’s asymmetric_flat4, which is not; a rotation’s braid, as σ₁σ₂ on three strands, is.

  • hash(Braid) agrees with ==: two words for one braid hash alike, so a braid is one key in a dict or a set however it is written. It hashed the word, while == compares the braid itself; it now hashes what every word for a braid shares (strands, exponent sum, permutation). word_eq still compares words.

  • The package says where braidpy lives: its repository, documentation, Braid Studio, changelog and issues (project.urls), for PyPI to show. Modules that began with a form (file name, author, date) say what they hold instead; horn_gear.multiband is in the API reference; and the documentation’s one link to a section, the marudai round a core, leads there.

  • Tightening warns when a braid is laid too coarsely for it: it looks for contact between samples only, so two yarns whose sideways steps between two levels add up to two diameters can cross there unseen, as the three-band machine’s did. A step of a diameter or more is warned of (take_off.SAFE_STEP, longest_step), for the page’s tightening too. Every machine is laid well inside it; a two-strand word settled by physics, and a lace braid made on a marudai round a core, are not.

  • Carriers no longer jump where a line of gears bends. On the three-band machine, folded round, and on the 12- and 16-carrier tubular braiders, a gear’s horns turned rigidly cannot meet both its contacts, so a carrier was drawn jumping, by up to a third of a gear, as it was handed on. The jump left yarns too long between samples for tightening to see one pass through another, and at the three-band machine’s seams, where the bands pull apart, the yarns twisted together there slipped through each other, so the bands came apart into three. Such a gear’s horns are now drawn spread unevenly, the one at a contact always on it (horn_gear.layout.slot_angles, GearAngles): carriers are handed on where they are, and the bands stay one braid when tightened. Which carrier crosses which is unchanged; machines whose horns met their contacts are drawn as before.

  • The 10-15-10 machine is loaded as its drawing is: each eight-horn gear carries one carrier each way, as drawn, where both went the same way (MULTIBAND_10_15_10_LOADING: G3 and G5 carry on slots 2 and 5, G10 and G12 on 1 and 4). Every other gear was already as drawn. The braid it makes changes; how the bands join does not.

  • Braid Studio stepped a big machine’s braid, the three-band machine’s, badly: the page drew the whole braid again at every frame, even when nothing moved, so it was slow to answer, and the handle, pressed a few times meanwhile, then turned on by itself. The view is drawn only while something changes, and a press the page could not answer in time is let go. The slider under the view goes a sub-step at a time, an eighth of a machine’s step or a disk’s move, the arrow keys a sub-step and with Shift a step, and says which step it is at.

  • Braid Studio’s machine handle, held part way through a step (▸), went back to the step’s start to turn it whole when pressed on, ▸ or ▶: it now turns on from where it is held.

  • A Braid Studio link could carry colours for strands its braid does not have: colours chosen on a braid of twenty strands stayed when one of sixteen was made after it, and its link listed all twenty. They are let go once the braid is made, and empty places after the last colour are not written.

  • Braids were taken for the same braid when their Alexander polynomial vanished and the rest of their fingerprint agreed: edo yatsu with ABOK #3070, and the flat braid of 6 with ABOK #3021. The fingerprint now holds the characteristic polynomial of the braid’s Burau matrix too (properties.burau_polynomial_on_circle), which tells them apart.

  • A braid with a long home braid, read from another starting point, could seem another braid: the Alexander polynomial was taken from the Burau matrix raised to the repeats, whose entries reach 1e19 for ABOK #3057, and lost the digits it cancelled. It is now taken from the matrix’s eigenvalues, as the Burau polynomial is.

  • A braid’s fingerprint took a minute when its home braid was hundreds of repeats of a long unit (the three-band machine: 210 repeats of 34515 crossings). Its linking numbers are now summed from the unit once, the repeats at once (linking.linking_matrix(braid, repeats)), and the Burau matrix is built a column update per crossing: five seconds.

  • Two words of one long braid could have fingerprints apart, their polynomials sampled in floating point: fingerprints now compare the Burau matrix’s characteristic polynomial computed exactly, modulo a prime (burau_mod).

  • A word of tens of thousands of crossings overflowed its floating-point Burau matrix, and fingerprint() raised: the matrix is scaled down as it grows.

  • A machine’s flat word missed a carrier passing several others in one sample, every later crossing given to the wrong strands; and carriers crossing just as a period starts were counted at neither end (flat_4’s word is twelve crossings, pure every period). A word is read over the loading’s own period: flat_9’s is 18 steps, not 180.

  • Yarns passing nearly the same point, as on one split axle, are lifted by the take-off rather than drawn out a hundredfold to part them.

  • A machine’s carrier track is drawn round its loop once: on a machine with a long period, the points kept fell laps apart and were joined across the deck.

Documentation

  • Laying out a horn gear machine: how gears are placed, sized and timed, with figures and equations.

0.3.0 — 2026-10-24

braidpy grows from braid words to the braids people make: on braiding and lace machines, kumihimo disks, a marudai and Ashley’s stand, laid and settled in 3D, compared by their topology, and shown in Braid Studio, in the browser.

New

  • Braid Studio, a web page where braidpy runs in the browser: describe a braid — a word, a kumihimo or mobidai pattern, an Ashley sinnet, a braiding or lace machine — and see it laid, tightened and made in 3D, with nothing installed. The braids it offers are made and settled with the site, and shown at once. A braid made on a disk can be saved for braid3dmin (B3D), bead for bead, its moves as braid3dmin lists them.

  • Braiding machines (braidpy.horn_gear): horn gear (maypole) machines, flat, tubular and soutache, and the Jacquard lace machine driven by its punchcard; their tracks, collisions, threading and animation, and their handle turned by hand.

  • Kumihimo and mobidai disks, animated from above, and 43 of Ashley’s solid sinnets (ABOK #2965–#3082), each worked on its stand, with its braid word and its mobidai pattern.

  • A braid made move by move on a marudai (braidpy.marudai), each yarn carried over the others and drawn tight.

  • Ways of making a braid (braidpy.making): from a braid word to how to make it — moves on a kumihimo disk, a Jacquard lace punchcard, a sinnet on Ashley’s stand — each checked by reading the braid back off what it makes.

  • Comparing braids (braidpy.similarity, braidpy.topology_catalog): a fingerprint that does not change however a braid is written, the braids most like one, and a catalogue of braids by topology with every way of making each.

  • The shape of a braid: laid in 3D as it comes off whatever made it, and tightened; closed forms for the shapes that have one (a laid rope, a braided tube, the flat braid); yarns settled by their own physics (braidpy.rope, braidpy.form), in Python and in the page.

  • For AI assistants: braidpy.tools, a few questions answered in plain data, and braidpy-mcp, an MCP server offering them (pip install "braidpy[mcp]").

  • Linking numbers, pure braids and their closures, full twists, the braid word read in any of its notations (braidword.parse_word).

  • braidpy.__version__.

Changed

These may need changes to code using 0.2.2.

  • Drawing is an extra: matplotlib, plotly and imageio are installed with pip install "braidpy[viz]"; pyqt6 and tornado are no longer required. braidpy alone installs in 100 MB rather than 489 MB, and a drawing library missing says what to install.

  • Python 3.10 to 3.15 are supported, and tested daily.

  • The 2D braid diagram is drawn by braidpy itself, in its own colours.

  • GarsideCanonicalFactors.n_half_twist counts half twists (it counted full ones).

  • Handle reduction (braidpy.handles_reduction) has a new interface.

  • Braid == and is_trivial() are decided by handle reduction, math-braid’s normal form only when it is slow: faster on most braids.

  • Drawing libraries, sympy and math-braid are imported only when used.

  • braidpy.ordered_strands, a stub nothing used, is removed.

  • double_flat3 makes ABOK 2971 by default; parallel=True, asking for ABOK 2972, raises NotImplementedError, as 2972 is not made yet.

Fixed

  • Comparing a braid with the trivial braid crashed; is_trivial() looked only at the word, so σ₁σ₁⁻¹ was not trivial.

  • Parametric strands stopped short of their end.

  • The documentation’s 3D example passed a braid where an amplitude was expected (issue #8).

  • Sinnets of more than 30 strands, made on a marudai, grew hundreds of yarn diameters long instead of a few.

  • double_flat3(parallel=True) gave ABOK 2971, not the 2972 it asked for.

  • braidpy-mcp without the mcp extra ended in a traceback; it now says what to install.

Documentation

  • In three parts: a user manual, a theory manual and a developer guide, with an API reference covering every module.
