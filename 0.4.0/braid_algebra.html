Braid algebra

Braids on n strands form a group, the braid group Bₙ: braids multiply by following one with the other, the trivial braid (no crossing) is the identity, and every braid has an inverse, its mirror image.

from braidpy import Braid

b = Braid([1, -2], n_strands=3)
b * b.inverse() == Braid([], n_strands=3)  # True
b**3  # b followed by itself twice more

Artin’s relations

Every braid is a product of the generators σ₁ … σₙ₋₁ and their inverses, and two words make the same braid exactly when one can be turned into the other by these relations (Artin, 1925):

  • Far crossings commute. σᵢσⱼ = σⱼσᵢ when |i − j| ≥ 2: crossings of four different strands can be done in either order.

  • The braid relation. σᵢσᵢ₊₁σᵢ = σᵢ₊₁σᵢσᵢ₊₁: three strands crossing each other once, in two orders, make the same braid.

  • And σᵢσᵢ⁻¹ is no crossing at all.

Braid([1, 3], n_strands=4) == Braid([3, 1], n_strands=4)  # True
Braid([1, 2, 1], n_strands=3) == Braid([2, 1, 2], n_strands=3)  # True

Deciding whether two words are the same braid

This is the word problem. a == b asks whether a·b⁻¹ is the trivial braid (is_trivial()), and braidpy answers that two ways:

  • Handle reduction (Dehornoy) removes handles — a σᵢ and a σᵢ⁻¹ (or the other way round) with neither σᵢ nor σᵢ₋₁ between them — until none is left; a word is the trivial braid exactly when it reduces to nothing (braidpy.handles_reduction). It is the faster on most braids, but no good bound on it is proven, and on long words of many strands it can blow up.

  • So it is given a few seconds, and if it has not decided, the braids’ Garside normal forms do (Braid representations), braidpy’s own (braidpy.garside_canonical_form): the word read with its half twists kept at its end, each factor made left-weighted with those before it in one pass back. ABOK #3082, 61 strands, against itself rewritten, takes three seconds so.

Shortening a word

A braid has many words, and the shorter is the fewer moves to make, by hand or on a machine. braid.shortened() (braidpy.shortening) gives the same braid, exactly, in as few crossings as it finds:

  • a crossing followed at once by its inverse is taken out;

  • a crossing and its inverse kept apart are brought together where the braid relations allow: crossings of strands far apart commute, and three crossings of three strands can be made the other way round, signs mixed too. Each piece of the word is rewritten every way they allow, up to a budget, for one where two crossings cancel;

  • handle reduction’s word is kept if it is shorter, after the same search. Removing every handle is not shortening: the book’s #3072 has 114 crossings, and 150 once its handles are reduced.

A word whose crossings all have one sign is as short as it gets, since the relations keep the sum of a word’s signs. A shortest word is not promised: finding one is hard in general. Braid Studio shows the shorter word under the braid’s own, when it finds one; the catalogue’s rectangular braid of 14 goes from 448 crossings to 364.

Twists and conjugates

  • The half twist Δ turns the whole braid over by half a turn (half_twist()), and the full twist Δ² by a whole turn (full_twist()). The full twist commutes with every braid: it generates the centre of the group. full_twists() counts how many a braid holds.

  • Conjugates. c b c⁻¹ is b seen after c (c * b * c.inverse()). Conjugate braids close into the same knot or link, which is why braids are compared up to conjugacy (braidpy.properties.conjugacy_class()).
